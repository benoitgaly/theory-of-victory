The observations

  1. 1er octobre 2021

    2,064.0Md$

    Medium carried by the engine

    The item is worth five years of what it produces. Out of $1,720bn of productive capacity, 24% is placed on the civilian side, that is $412.8bn a year, which capitalisation raises to $2,064bn. This is not the whole non-military economy — services, agriculture and the arms plants are excluded from it — but it is by far the widest of the seven bases, and that is what makes it the largest item on the balance sheet.

    Two conventions of the game, owned as such: the civilian share of the productive apparatus, set at 24%, and capitalisation at five years of output. Neither of the two is measured; only their input, GDP, is.

  2. 2020

    4.0% du PIB · estimation CIA

    Low

    The pre-war baseline. The CIA and SIPRI agree here to within a tenth, which is reassuring about both.

    An estimate by an intelligence service, published with no method and no interval. Its worth lies in its continuity, not in its precision — hence the rounding to the whole point.

  3. 2021

    1,829.2Md$

    High

    Whole GDP is not what this item carries, but it is where everything starts. The scenario opens on a rounded figure of $1,800bn, of which 1,720 is productive capacity.

    A measurement published by the World Bank, read off its data interface, with no conversion and no interpolation.

  4. 2021

    144.7M hab.

    High

    Population enters into no calculation in the engine. It is cited here because it answers the question the banner asks without meaning to: the gap between the two civilian economies is one third a matter of the number of inhabitants and two thirds a matter of what each of them produces.

    A United Nations estimate carried by the World Bank. The uncertainty bears on the attachment of Crimea, not on the order of magnitude.

  5. 2021

    High carried by the engine

    A value read directly in table 1 of the fact sheet, column “Spending as a share of GDP (%) 2021”, Russia row.

    The figure is indeed attributed to Rosstat by name and dated to the day of its publication, but I was unable to open the Rosstat publication itself (the TLS certificate of rosstat.gov.ru cannot be verified and the site returns an empty page to scraping), and Rosstat has revised this series since.

  9. 2021

    3,407.0Md ₽ · budget amendé

    Low

    Not to be used as a reference for 2021: the observed outturn for the same year, by contrast, is certain and comes to 3,573.6 billion (see the next line). The gap of 166 billion between the two gives the order of magnitude of in-year drift in peacetime — drift that will become incomparably larger from 2022 onwards.

    The value is read unambiguously on the page rendered as an image, but the brochure attaches no note to its 2021 column: it is impossible to say whether it carries the initial 2021 budget act or a position amended during the financial year, hence the cautious label.

  10. 2021

  11. 2022

    4.1% du PIB

    High

    86.4 billion dollars, +9.2% in real terms. The ratio is stable against 2021 because Russian nominal GDP rose sharply in 2022 under the effect of hydrocarbon prices: the stability of the ratio masks a real increase in spending.

    Table 1, 2022 column, value in square brackets [4.1] marking a SIPRI estimate, which the fact sheet documents explicitly.

  12. 2022

    18.0% du PIB

    Medium

    A cyclical peak tied to hydrocarbon prices in 2022. A much-quoted quarterly figure for that year — 21.7% in the first quarter of 2022, a record since the calculations began — is confirmed by INTERFAX-2022-07-20, but it is a quarter, not a year, and confusing it with the year inflates the estimate by close to 4 points.

    A figure attributed to Rosstat in a dated article, but read in a press report rather than in the original publication; other reports give 18.1% for the same year, which reflects successive revisions.

    An estimate made at the time when the Russian budget was ceasing to be published by section: it rests on sources the agency does not set out.

    High

  33. 2025

    6.3% du PIB

    Medium

    The 2025 nominal GDP figure comes from source ANGI-2026-04-20. To be kept as an order of magnitude: the “National Defence” budget line alone is worth about 6.3% of GDP, against 7.5% for the SIPRI measure, which additionally covers military pensions, part of the paramilitary forces and militarised spending lodged in other sections. The gap between the two measures is therefore about 1.2 points of GDP, and it runs in the expected direction.

    A ratio I calculated myself — 13,503.7 billion roubles measured against 2025 nominal GDP of 214.3 trillion roubles published by Rosstat — and not a figure published as such by an institution.

  34. 2025

    13.0% du PIB

    Medium

    An all-time low since Rosstat's calculations began in 2017, against 14% at the previous trough in 2020. The non-oil-and-gas sector reaches 87% of GDP.
